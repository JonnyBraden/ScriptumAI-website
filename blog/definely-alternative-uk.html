Definely alternative for mid-sized UK law firms

By Jonny Braden·Published 8 November 2026·3 min read
The short answer

Definely is an excellent tool for reading, navigating and drafting complex agreements in Microsoft Word, and it is widely used at the largest UK firms. Scriptum is the better alternative for a mid-sized UK transactional firm that wants the whole deal covered: first drafts from your own sources, review of the other side's markup for partner sign-off, negotiation inside partner-set limits, a client and opposing counsel workspace, and published pricing from £79 per user per month.

Definely is a UK legal technology company founded by former Freshfields lawyers. It raised a $30m Series B in June 2025, led by Revaia, and UKTN reported more than 100 law firm and in-house customers at the time, including A&O Shearman, Slaughter and May, DLA Piper and Dentons. Its products run inside Microsoft Word.

What is Definely?

Definely builds Word add-ins for drafting, reviewing and accessing legal knowledge. Legal Technology Hub lists Draft for contextual editing (navigating definitions and cross-references inside a document), Vault for inserting clauses and Enhance for AI drafting assistance. Enhance, a multi-agent AI system, launched in May 2025. Definely describes its platform as built for lawyers working under pressure on complex, high-stakes deals.

Two different jobs

Definely is very good at making a long, dense agreement easier to work with: jumping to definitions, checking cross-references and getting the document right. That matters, and we have written about the defined-terms headache ourselves.

Scriptum is built for a different job: running the deal. It starts the first draft from your template, the term sheet and the email chain. It reviews each round of the other side's markup against your playbook. On Pro, it answers inside the limits your partner has set. And it records what was agreed, so the firm keeps it.

Why Scriptum is the better Definely alternative for mid-sized firms

  • Built for firms your size. Pricing, onboarding and features are designed for firms of 20 to 100 fee earners, without a knowledge team to run it.
  • Covers the negotiation. Review for partner sign-off, negotiation inside limits and a running issues list across every turn.
  • Brings the client and the other side in. Clients comment on the clause itself, and their feedback becomes proposed drafting that waits for your approval.
  • Published pricing. You can see the cost before the first call.

When Definely may suit you better

  • Your main pain is navigating and checking very long agreements rather than running negotiations.
  • Your firm already uses Definely and you want to stay inside one vendor's Word tools.
