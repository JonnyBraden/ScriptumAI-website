Managing hallucination risk in legal AI

15 August 2026 · 3 min read

Managing hallucination risk in legal AI is the question that most often determines whether a transactional firm takes AI-assisted drafting seriously or dismisses it. The concern is legitimate and the risk is real, but it is also frequently overstated in ways that obscure the more practical question of how architectural choices reduce the risk to an acceptable level.

What hallucination means in a drafting context

Hallucination in a general-purpose language model means the model generates content that is plausible-sounding but factually incorrect, fabricated or inconsistent with the source material. In a research or legal advice context, this is a serious risk: a model that invents case citations or misrepresents statutory provisions produces outputs that are actively dangerous.

In a transactional drafting context, the hallucination risk is different. Drafting tasks are largely about structure, clause selection and language, not about recalling external facts. The question is not whether the AI has accurately stated what a court decided; it is whether the AI has produced a clause that reflects the firm's standard position on, say, limitation of liability in a services agreement. That is a very different kind of task, with a different risk profile.

How architectural choices reduce the risk

The hallucination risk in a drafting tool is primarily managed through the architecture of how the AI draws on source material. A tool that generates clauses from a general model trained on internet text carries a higher hallucination risk than a tool that drafts by retrieving and adapting clauses from the firm's own verified precedents. The second approach grounds the output in material the firm knows is correct, which fundamentally changes the nature of the risk.

This is why the question of whether a legal AI tool is general-purpose or precedent-grounded matters so much in a risk assessment. Grounding the output in verified firm documents is the single most important architectural safeguard against hallucination in a drafting context.

Supervision as the final layer

The other layer of hallucination management is human supervision. AI-generated drafts should be reviewed by a qualified lawyer before they leave the firm. This is not different in principle from the supervision obligation that applies to any associate-produced work. The review is more targeted because the risk profile is different, but it remains the final quality control layer.
