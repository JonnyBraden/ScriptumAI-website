What security questions should a law firm ask a legal AI vendor?

Published 12 November 2026·5 min read
The short answer

The security questions a law firm should ask a legal AI vendor are: where client data is stored and processed, whether it is used to train AI models, how your data is kept separate from other customers', which certifications the vendor holds, who can access your data, and what happens to it when you leave. Get the answers in writing and check that the contract says the same thing as the website.

Most firms of 20 to 100 fee earners do not have a security team to run vendor due diligence. The partner sponsoring the tool, the person responsible for risk and compliance and whoever looks after IT usually share it. This checklist is meant to give them a common list, so every vendor on the shortlist answers the same questions.

What security questions should you ask a legal AI vendor?

Ask the eight questions below of every vendor, whether that is Harvey, Legora, Spellbook, Luminance, Microsoft Copilot or Scriptum AI. Comparing written answers side by side tells you far more than a sales demo.

Where is our data stored, and where is it processed?Storage and processing can happen in different countries, and international transfers need a lawful basisNamed locations for both, with the transfer mechanism stated

A starting checklist, not legal advice. Add your clients' and panels' own security requirements.

Why does it matter where legal AI processes data?

Because storage and processing are often in different places. A vendor can store documents in the UK or EU while sending text to an AI model provider elsewhere for processing.

That is not necessarily a problem, but you need to know it, check the transfer mechanism and reflect it in your data protection assessment. The ICO's guidance on AI and data protection is the place to start, and our post on GDPR and legal AI covers what firms usually need to do.

Should a law firm let a vendor train AI on its documents?

For client work, generally no. Your precedents and negotiated positions are confidential to your clients and valuable to your firm, and they should not improve a model that your competitors also use.

Check that the commitment covers the vendor's own AI providers as well as the vendor, and that it sits in the contract. The SRA's Risk Outlook report on the use of AI in the legal market is a useful read on confidentiality and AI more broadly, alongside our post on keeping client data confidential when using AI.

A vendor's security page is a promise. The contract is what you can rely on.

Do certifications like ISO 27001 and SOC 2 settle it?

They help, but they do not answer every question on the list. A certificate shows a vendor runs an audited security programme; it does not tell you where your documents are processed or whether they are used for training.

Younger vendors may still be working towards certification. That is worth knowing rather than a reason to stop, provided they say so honestly and answer your questionnaire in writing. The useful test is whether the vendor's answer matches what your clients and panels will accept.

What should an in-house legal team add?

Your company's own procurement and information security requirements. In-house teams rarely choose a vendor alone, so bring IT security in at the start rather than after the pilot.

The questions above still apply, and in-house teams should also check how the tool fits the company's Microsoft 365 set-up. Our guide to the best AI drafting tool for in-house legal teams covers the wider choice.

Common questions

Is legal AI safe to use with client documents?

It can be, if the vendor stores data in a known location, does not train models on it, separates your data from other customers' and lets you delete it when you leave. Ask for those commitments in writing, in the contract, not only on a web page.

Should a legal AI vendor have ISO 27001 or SOC 2?

Independent certification is a strong signal, but it is not the only one. If a vendor is not yet certified, ask what stage it has reached and for written answers to your security questionnaire, and weigh that against your clients' and panels' requirements.

Do we need a data protection impact assessment for legal AI?

Often, yes. If the tool will process personal data in client documents, check whether UK GDPR requires a data protection impact assessment before a pilot on live matters. The ICO's guidance on AI and data protection explains when one is needed.

Do in-house legal teams need to ask different questions?

Mostly the same questions, plus your company's own information security and procurement requirements. In-house teams should involve IT security early, because they will usually have to approve the vendor anyway.
