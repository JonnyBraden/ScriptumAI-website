Why transparent pricing matters when choosing legal AI

15 August 2026 · 3 min read

Why transparent pricing matters when choosing legal AI is not purely a question of budget planning. It is a signal about how a vendor thinks about smaller firms and what the relationship will look like once you are already committed to the contract.

The opacity problem in legal tech

Pricing for legal AI tools is frequently unavailable without a sales conversation. You fill in a contact form, sit through a demonstration, receive a proposal and only then discover the per-user cost, the implementation fee, the minimum seat requirement and the contract length. By that point, you have invested time, raised expectations internally and begun to anchor on the product as the answer.

This is not accidental. Opaque pricing is a sales tactic. It forces engagement, creates artificial urgency and makes it harder to compare options objectively.

What opaque pricing signals about fit

For a firm of 20 to 100 lawyers, the practical consequence is that you may be several weeks into an evaluation before you discover that the tool is priced for a much larger organisation. The per-seat cost assumes a hundred-plus users to be viable, the implementation requires dedicated technology resource you do not have, and the contract length assumes an enterprise procurement cycle.

A vendor whose pricing structure is built for large firms will, consciously or not, build a product for large firms. The feature set, the onboarding assumptions and the support model all follow the money. A firm at the smaller end of the market tends to get a product designed for someone else, at a price point that was not designed for them either.

What transparent pricing looks like

Scriptum publishes its founding-firm pricing at 69 pounds per user per month. There are no implementation fees added on top, no minimum seat thresholds that push the cost out of reach and no requirement to commit to a multi-year term before you have seen the tool work for your practice. You can assess the financial case, compare it against alternatives and make a decision without sitting through a sales process designed to make objective comparison difficult.
