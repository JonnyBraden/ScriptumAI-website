Why transparent pricing matters when choosing legal AI

3 August 2026 · 3 min read · Jonny Braden

Transparent pricing matters when choosing legal AI because opaque pricing tells you something important about who the tool was built for, and it is probably not you.

Most enterprise legal AI tools do not publish prices. You book a call, describe your firm, wait for a scoping conversation, and eventually receive a proposal calibrated to what the vendor thinks you will pay. This process can take weeks. It also means the price you receive reflects your negotiating position relative to the vendor's sales team, not the actual value of the product to your practice.

What the opacity signals

Enterprise pricing models exist because large law firms with large IT budgets and long procurement cycles will pay more than smaller firms. Vendors who sell into both segments charge different prices to different buyers for the same product, and they need opacity to maintain those differences. If the price were published, the gap would be obvious.

For a transactional firm with 20 to 100 fee earners, this matters in two ways. First, you are likely paying a significant premium relative to larger buyers who have more leverage in the negotiation. Second, the product has probably been designed and roadmapped for those larger buyers. Your needs, your workflow, and your capacity constraints are secondary considerations. You are purchasing a product whose development priorities do not reflect yours.

What transparent pricing looks like in practice

Transparent pricing is a number on the website. Not a range, not a "starting from", not a form to fill in before you receive anything. A number. Scriptum's founding-firm pricing is £69 per user per month. You can assess the commercial case before you speak to anyone. If it works for your firm's economics, you know. If it does not, you have not spent two weeks on a sales process to reach that conclusion.

That transparency has a second function beyond commercial honesty. It signals that the vendor is comfortable with its own value proposition. If the price is published, the value has to be clear enough to justify it without a sales pitch to soften the number.

For firms evaluating legal AI tools, the presence or absence of transparent pricing is one of the fastest signals available about whether the vendor is genuinely building for your segment or using smaller firms to subsidise a larger customer base.
