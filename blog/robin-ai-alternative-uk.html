Robin AI alternative: options for UK legal teams in 2026

By Jonny Braden·Published 8 November 2026·4 min read
The short answer

Robin AI was put up for distressed sale in October 2025 after failing to raise new funding. Its managed services team moved to Scissero in December 2025, and Microsoft was reported in January 2026 to be hiring its remaining technology team. For UK law firms that used Robin AI for contract drafting and review, Scriptum is a strong replacement: it works in Word, reviews the other side's markup for partner sign-off, keeps a clause library your firm owns, and publishes its prices.

Robin AI was a legal AI company with offices in London and New York that combined contract AI software with managed legal services. It was one of the better-known names in UK legal tech, which is why its collapse caught many people by surprise.

What happened to Robin AI?

  • October 2025: Robin AI was put up for distressed sale after failing to raise funding, according to Legal IT Insider.
  • December 2025: Scissero, an AI-enabled law firm, acquired Robin AI's managed services team. Legal IT Insider reported that Robin's chief executive was not joining Scissero.
  • January 2026: Artificial Lawyer reported that Microsoft was set to acqui-hire Robin AI's remaining technology team, to help improve Word for lawyers.

The reports we reviewed do not confirm what happened to every customer contract or to the Robin AI software itself. If you were a customer, ask Scissero directly what applies to you.

What former Robin AI users should check now

  1. Your data. Where are your documents held today, who controls them, and how do you get a full export?
  2. Your contract. Has it been assigned, and to whom? What happens to any prepaid fees?
  3. Your playbooks and precedents. Anything you built inside the tool may be hard to retrieve. Get it out in a usable format while you can.

The lesson: make sure the knowledge is yours

Robin AI's story is a reminder that legal AI vendors can fail, be acquired or change direction. The tool is replaceable. What your firm learned while using it may not be, unless your contract says it belongs to you and you can take it with you.

That is why Scriptum's terms make the clause library firm-owned on every plan. The clauses your lawyers agree, and their negotiation history, belong to the firm, not to us.
