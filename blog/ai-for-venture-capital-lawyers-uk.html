AI for venture capital and startup investment lawyers in the UK

Published 8 November 2026·4 min read
The short answer

For UK venture capital lawyers, the most useful legal AI drafts the investment documents from the term sheet and your own precedents, reviews the investor's or company's markup against your positions, and remembers what each fund and counterparty has accepted before. Scriptum is built for exactly this, inside Microsoft Word, with partner sign-off on every point and a clause library your firm owns.

I spent several years as external counsel to a venture capital fund before founding Scriptum, and VC work is where the case for legal AI is clearest. The documents are familiar. The negotiation points repeat. The same funds and the same lawyers sit on the other side again and again. And most of what a firm learns from each round is lost in an email chain.

Which VC documents can AI help with?

  • Term sheets: the source the rest of the documents must follow.
  • Subscription agreements: warranties, limitations, conditions and completion mechanics.
  • Shareholders' agreements: investor consents, information rights, leaver provisions, drag and tag.
  • Articles of association: share rights, anti-dilution, liquidation preference and transfer provisions.
  • Disclosure letters and the ancillary documents around completion.

Many UK deals start from the BVCA model documents, but every firm and every fund has its own variations. Those variations are exactly what a generic AI tool does not know.

Where AI makes the biggest difference on a VC deal

Turning the term sheet into first drafts

Scriptum's drafting agent takes your template, the agreed term sheet and the email chain, and produces first drafts that reflect the commercial deal. It flags every place where the sources disagree, such as a valuation in an email that does not match the term sheet, so a lawyer decides rather than the model guessing.

Reviewing the other side's markup

The review agent reads the investor's or company's turn against your playbook and your client's priorities, and builds a review plan a partner can sign off point by point.

Negotiating within the partner's limits

On the Pro plan, the negotiation agent answers each round inside the limits your partner has set: for example, how far you will move on warranty caps or leaver definitions. Anything outside those limits goes back to the partner.

Remembering the fund

Every clause you agree is captured with its negotiation history, and the Pro plan builds a counterparty history. The next time the same fund's lawyers send a markup, your team knows what they accepted last time.

Why a specialist tool beats a general one for VC work

A general AI model can write a plausible shareholders' agreement. It cannot tell you what your firm agreed with a particular fund last quarter, or which fallback on good leaver your partner is happy to give. That knowledge is the real value in a VC practice, and it should belong to the firm. Scriptum is built to capture it.

Common questions

Can AI draft a shareholders' agreement?

AI can produce a strong first draft, especially from your own precedents and the agreed term sheet. A lawyer should review and approve every clause. Scriptum's drafting agent works this way, flagging conflicts between the sources for a lawyer to resolve.

What is the best AI tool for venture capital lawyers in the UK?

For UK VC lawyers in mid-sized firms, Scriptum is a strong fit because it drafts from term sheets and your own precedents in Word, reviews markup for partner sign-off and remembers what each counterparty has accepted.

Does AI work with BVCA model documents?

Yes. If your firm's templates are based on the BVCA model documents, a tool that drafts from your own precedents will follow your version of them, including your firm's usual variations.
