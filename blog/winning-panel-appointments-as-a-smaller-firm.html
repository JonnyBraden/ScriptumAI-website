Winning panel appointments as a smaller firm

2 July 2026 · 3 min read

Winning panel appointments as a smaller firm has become a more realistic objective in the past few years as corporate legal departments have begun to unbundle their work and allocate specific transaction types to specialist advisers rather than routing everything through a single large firm relationship.

What panel selection actually involves

Corporate legal departments conducting a panel review are evaluating several things simultaneously. Technical capability in the relevant practice area is a threshold requirement, but it rarely differentiates candidates who have cleared the initial screen. The questions that drive the final decision tend to be about responsiveness, consistency across the team, process maturity and, increasingly, the technology infrastructure the firm has in place.

A smaller firm that can demonstrate that its associates produce drafts to the same standard as its partners, that it can turn around time-sensitive documents within client expectations and that it has a structured approach to managing deal knowledge is making a compelling case. Size becomes less relevant when the operational questions are answered well.

The technology question

Panel pitches from smaller firms increasingly include questions about the firm's approach to legal technology. This is partly about efficiency and partly about risk: a corporate client whose transactions depend on tight timetables wants to know that the firm has thought about how it manages document production, version control and counterparty review.

Firms that can point to a genuine technology infrastructure, rather than a commitment to exploring options, are in a better position to answer this question convincingly. The pitch is not about the tool; it is about what the tool enables the firm to deliver to that client specifically.

Making the case on deliverables

The most persuasive panel pitches for smaller transactional firms are built around specific deliverables: what the firm will produce, how quickly, in what format and with what consistency. Concrete commitments on turnaround times for standard documents, a demonstrated approach to counterparty review and a clear escalation process for complex issues all translate into the kind of operational picture that a corporate legal department can evaluate and compare.
