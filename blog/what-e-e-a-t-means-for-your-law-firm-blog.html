What E-E-A-T means for your law firm blog

14 June 2026 · 3 min read

What E-E-A-T means for your law firm blog is a question with real commercial significance: Google's quality evaluation framework, which stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness, applies particularly strictly to legal content because legal topics are categorised as Your Money or Your Life, a category where Google holds content to a higher standard.

Why legal content is held to a higher standard

Google's search quality guidelines treat legal content as a sensitive category because poor-quality or inaccurate legal information can cause real harm. A user who relies on misleading legal guidance may make decisions that have significant consequences. As a result, Google's evaluation of legal content goes beyond technical SEO factors and looks at whether the content is produced by people with genuine expertise, whether the source is authoritative in the field and whether the content can be trusted to be accurate.

For a law firm blog, this means that thin, generic or obviously non-expert content is likely to underperform in search regardless of how well it is optimised at the technical level. The quality signals matter more than the keyword density.

What experience and expertise mean in practice

Experience, in the E-E-A-T framework, refers to first-hand knowledge of the subject. A law firm blog post that draws on the firm's actual practice, deals the team has handled or questions clients regularly ask demonstrates experience in a way that a generic overview of a legal topic does not. The distinction is between content that could have been written by anyone with access to a textbook and content that could only have been written by someone who has actually done the work.

Expertise refers to demonstrated professional competence. For a law firm, this is addressed partly through the quality and accuracy of the content and partly through signals that the content comes from a qualified professional source: the firm's credentials, the practice area it operates in and the specificity of the guidance it provides.

Authoritativeness and trustworthiness

Authoritativeness is built over time through consistent publication of high-quality, accurate content and through external recognition: links from other professional sources, mentions in credible publications and a track record that signals expertise to both Google and readers. Trustworthiness is supported by accuracy, transparency about the firm's credentials and clear attribution of the source of the content. A firm that publishes content that is reliably correct and practically useful builds trust with both readers and search engines over time.
