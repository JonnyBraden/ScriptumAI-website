How can private equity lawyers use AI to draft deal documents?

Published 27 November 2026·6 min read
The short answer

Private equity lawyers can use AI to produce first drafts of the documents that repeat on every buyout: share purchase agreements, investment agreements, articles, management equity documents, disclosure letters and ancillaries. It works best when the AI drafts from the firm's own precedents inside Microsoft Word, and a lawyer reviews every clause before it goes to the sponsor or the other side.

Private equity deal teams live with fixed timetables, sponsors who expect the same positions on every deal, and a document list that runs to dozens of items. That makes PE one of the transactional practices where AI drafting pays off fastest. This guide is for corporate and private equity lawyers at mid-sized firms in the UK, Ireland, the USA and Canada, and for in-house lawyers at funds and portfolio companies. It sets out where AI for private equity lawyers helps, where it does not, and how to choose a tool.

Where does AI help private equity lawyers most?

On first drafts of precedent-heavy documents. Most of a buyout document list starts from a template the firm has used before, adjusted for the deal; AI can do that adjustment in minutes rather than hours.

The gains are less about clever drafting and more about removing the slow, mechanical work that sits between heads of terms and a draft fit for partner review: carrying the deal terms through, conforming defined terms, updating parties and cross-references, and pulling the sponsor's usual positions from the last deal.

Which PE deal documents can AI draft well?

The ones that follow a firm precedent closely. The table below is a practical view of where AI drafting tends to save time on a typical buyout, and where a lawyer still has to lead.

DocumentHow well AI drafts itWhere the lawyer must lead
Share purchase agreementStrong first draft from the firm's SPA precedent and heads of termsPrice mechanics, warranty and indemnity package, limitations
Investment agreementStrong, especially where the sponsor's positions repeat deal to dealInvestor consents, leaver provisions, commercial deviations
Articles of associationStrong, conformed to the investment agreementShare rights and ratchets that are bespoke to the deal
Management equity and leaver documentsGood, from the firm's precedentsTax-sensitive terms, agreed with tax colleagues
Disclosure letter and ancillariesGood for structure, board minutes and routine ancillariesSpecific disclosures, which depend on diligence
Negotiation markupsUseful for redlines and issues lists against agreed positionsJudgement on what to concede and when

A practitioner's view, not a benchmark. Results depend on the quality of the firm's precedents.

What are the risks of using AI on a private equity deal?

Confidentiality and accuracy. Deal information is price-sensitive, and a draft that looks right can still carry the wrong mechanics or a stale position.

Before any pilot, confirm the vendor does not train models on your data, where it stores it and who its sub-processors are; our list of security questions to ask a legal AI vendor covers this. Then treat every AI draft as you would a trainee's: check defined terms, cross-references, the price mechanism and governing law first. Our guide on how to check an AI-drafted contract has a step-by-step list.

On a buyout, the value of AI is not a clever clause. It is a clean first draft of forty documents that already know what the sponsor agreed last time.

What should a mid-sized firm look for in AI for private equity work?

A tool that fits how PE deals are actually run. In practice, check these five things.

  1. It works in Microsoft Word. PE documents are drafted and negotiated in Word, with tracked changes passing between several sets of advisers.
  2. It drafts from your own precedents. Your SPA, investment agreement and articles carry positions the firm and its sponsors have already agreed.
  3. It remembers positions across deals. Sponsors expect consistency; the tool should capture what was agreed so the next deal starts there.
  4. A lawyer approves everything. Nothing goes to the client or the other side without sign-off.
  5. The pricing is clear. You should be able to model the cost per lawyer before you commit.

Do private equity lawyers need an all-purpose legal AI platform?

Not always. Broad platforms such as Harvey and Legora cover research, litigation and drafting, and both now market to smaller and mid-sized firms. They suit firms that want one tool across every practice.

A transactional team that mainly needs faster, consistent first drafts may be better served by a tool built for that job. Key takeaways

  • AI for private equity lawyers is most useful on first drafts of precedent-heavy deal documents.
  • SPAs, investment agreements, articles and ancillaries are strong candidates; bespoke economics stay with the lawyer.

Common questions

Can AI draft a share purchase agreement for a private equity deal?

It can produce a strong first draft if it works from your firm's own SPA precedents and the agreed heads of terms. A lawyer still has to check the commercial terms, the locked box or completion accounts mechanics, defined terms and cross-references before it goes to the other side.

Is AI useful for investment agreements and articles on a buyout?

Yes. Investment agreements, articles, management equity documents and loan note instruments are heavily precedent-driven and repeat across a sponsor's deals, which is where AI drafting from the firm's own templates saves the most time.

Is it safe to put confidential deal information into a legal AI tool?

Only if the vendor contractually commits not to train models on your data and can tell you where it is stored and who its sub-processors are. Check this before a pilot, not after, as you would for any supplier handling price-sensitive information.

Do mid-sized firms need Harvey or Legora for private equity work?

Not necessarily. Harvey and Legora are broad platforms that also cover research and litigation and now market to smaller firms too. A transactional team that mainly needs faster first drafts in Word may get more from a drafting tool built for that work.
