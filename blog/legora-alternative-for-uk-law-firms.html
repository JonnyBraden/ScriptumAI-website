Legora alternative for mid-sized UK law firms

The short answer

For a mid-sized UK firm focused on transactional work, Scriptum is the best Legora alternative. It is built only for drafting, reviewing and negotiating documents, negotiates inside limits your partners set, captures a clause library your firm owns, and publishes its prices from £79 per user per month. Legora is a Swedish legal AI company and Harvey's closest competitor. Legal Business reported in October 2026 that it had raised $550m at an $8.8bn valuation. Like Harvey, it now pitches directly to boutique and mid-size firms, promising deployment in days rather than quarters.

It is a serious product. The question for a UK firm of 20 to 100 fee earners is whether its breadth matches the work you do.

What is Legora?

Legora is a collaborative legal AI platform. Its page for small and mid-sized firms features an Agent that runs complex work end-to-end, Tabular Review for pulling data out of large document sets, a Word add-in that applies a firm's playbooks to documents, an Outlook add-in and a Portal. It also offers regulatory monitoring and lists. Legora lists SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and ISO 42001 among its security credentials.

Legora does not publish prices on its website.

What is the difference between a playbook and a clause library?

This is the most useful distinction to understand when comparing the two.

A playbook tells the AI what your firm's positions are: we accept a cap at the contract price, we never give an uncapped indemnity, and so on. Someone has to write it and keep it up to date.

A clause library with negotiation history records what your firm actually agreed: the wording that went out, what the other side changed, which fallback was accepted, and on which matter. It builds itself as your lawyers work. Over time, it tells you what the market will actually accept, and what a particular counterparty usually concedes.

Scriptum does both. On the Pro plan, the firm playbook and counterparty history feed the negotiation agent, so it argues from what your firm has really agreed before.
