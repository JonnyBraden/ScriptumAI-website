Can you train legal AI on your law firm's own precedents?

Published 10 November 2026·4 min read
The short answer

Can you train legal AI on your law firm's own precedents? Yes, but usually not in the way the phrase suggests: most legal AI tools do not retrain a model on your firm's precedents; they ground each draft in them, retrieving your own templates and agreed clauses and drafting from those. For a law firm, grounding is usually what you want: the output reads like your firm, and your documents never become part of someone else's model.

"Can we train it on our own precedents?" is one of the first questions partners ask when they see legal AI drafting a contract. It is the right instinct. A first draft that reads like a stranger wrote it saves very little time, because someone still has to pull it back to the firm's house style and standard positions. The useful question is how a tool uses your precedents, and who owns what it learns.

What does "training AI on your precedents" actually mean?

It can mean two quite different things. Fine-tuning changes a model's underlying weights using your documents. Grounding, often called retrieval, leaves the model unchanged and instead gives it the relevant parts of your precedent bank each time it drafts.

Why does grounding suit a law firm better?

Because a firm's precedents change and its lawyers need to check the source. When the corporate team updates its standard warranty time limits, a grounded tool can use the new position straight away, and the reviewing lawyer can see which precedent a suggestion came from. That is easier to supervise than wording that emerges from inside a model.

The point is not that the AI learns your precedents. It is that your firm keeps what it learns.

What should you check before giving a tool your precedent bank?

Treat it as you would any supplier handling client material. These are the questions we would ask any vendor, including us.

  1. Is our data isolated? Your precedents should be kept separate from other customers' data.
  2. Is it used for training? Get a written commitment that your documents are not used to train models, and that it applies to the vendor's model providers too.
  3. Can we see the source? Each suggestion should show which precedent or agreed clause it draws on.
  4. Who maintains it? A precedent bank that relies on someone filing documents tends to go stale.
  5. Can we take it with us? You should be able to export your library at any time.

Do Harvey, Legora and Copilot work with your own documents?

Broadly, yes. Harvey, Legora and Microsoft Copilot all let users work with their own documents in some form, and each is a capable product. They are built to cover a wide range of legal work, including litigation and research. If your firm's work is mainly transactional, the question is whether a tool turns your precedents and negotiated positions into a structured, reusable record, or simply lets you search and summarise files. Common questions

Can I train ChatGPT or another AI model on my firm's precedents?

You can upload documents to some general tools, but that is not the same as a drafting tool built around your precedent bank. For client work, check where the documents are stored, whether they are used for training, and whether the output follows your house style.

Is it safe to give legal AI access to our precedents?

It can be, if the vendor keeps your data isolated from other customers, commits in writing not to use it to train models, and lets you export or delete it. Ask for those commitments before uploading anything.

Do we need a knowledge management team to do this?

No. A tool that captures clause choices as lawyers draft and negotiate builds the library as a byproduct of normal work. Someone should still own the firm's standard positions.
