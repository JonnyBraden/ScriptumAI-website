What is the best AI tool for M&A lawyers in the UK?

Published 23 November 2026·6 min read
The short answer

The best AI tool for M&A lawyers in the UK is the one that works on the documents a deal team actually lives in: the share purchase agreement, the disclosure letter, ancillaries and each turn of the other side's markup. For most mid-sized corporate teams that means a tool inside Microsoft Word that drafts from the firm's own precedents, redlines incoming markup and keeps a running issues list, with a separate due diligence tool only if review volumes justify it.

M&A is one of the most document-heavy areas of legal practice, and it is where legal AI vendors have pitched hardest. Harvey, Legora, Luminance, Spellbook, Definely, Microsoft Copilot and Scriptum AI all claim a role on a deal. They do not all do the same job. This guide sets out what an M&A team at a mid-sized firm in the UK or Ireland should expect from AI at each stage of a transaction, and how to choose.

What can AI do on an M&A deal?

AI can now help at almost every stage of a deal, but the value is concentrated in three places: due diligence, first drafts and the negotiation rounds. Each needs a different kind of tool.

In due diligence, AI reads large volumes of target contracts and flags provisions such as change of control, assignment, exclusivity and termination rights. In drafting, it turns a term sheet and the firm's precedents into a first draft of the SPA and ancillaries. In negotiation, it compares the other side's markup against your draft and your playbook, and drafts responses for a lawyer to approve.

What should an M&A team look for in a legal AI tool?

Look for a tool that matches how deals are actually run, rather than the longest feature list. Use this checklist in demos:

  1. Does it work inside Microsoft Word? SPAs and disclosure letters are negotiated in Word, with tracked changes. A tool that makes lawyers copy text in and out of a browser will not be used under deal pressure.
  2. Does it draft from your precedents? Your warranty package, limitation of liability schedule and completion mechanics reflect years of agreed positions. Drafts should start there, not from generic market language.
  3. Can it handle the markup rounds? Ask to see it redline a real incoming markup and produce an issues list across turns, not just a single clause.
  4. Does a partner sign off? Nothing should go to the client or the other side without a lawyer approving it.
  5. Who owns the knowledge? Positions agreed on each deal should build a clause library and playbook that belongs to the firm, not the vendor.
  6. Is the price clear? Ask what a seat costs, what it includes and whether usage is capped.
  7. Are the security answers in writing? Deal documents are price-sensitive. Get commitments on model training, data separation and retention into the contract.

Do M&A lawyers need an enterprise platform like Harvey or Legora?

Some do, many do not. Harvey and Legora are capable broad platforms, and Legora now markets to small and mid-sized firms as well as large ones, so size alone is not the test.

The test is fit. A firm that wants one tool for litigation, research and corporate work may value a broad platform. A corporate team whose day is drafting and negotiating may get more from a tool built for that work. It is Scriptum's view that on broad per-seat platforms, transactional users effectively subsidise the heavier usage of litigation work; we set out that argument in should transactional firms pay for all-purpose legal AI? On a deal, the hours go on the markup rounds, so judge an AI tool by how it handles turn three, not the first draft.

Common questions

Can AI draft a share purchase agreement?

AI can produce a strong first draft of a share purchase agreement from a term sheet and the firm's own precedents, but a qualified lawyer must still review it, settle the commercial points and take responsibility for the final document. Treat the output as a junior's first turn, not a finished draft.

Is AI good at M&A due diligence?

AI is well suited to the first pass of due diligence: reading large volumes of contracts and flagging change of control, assignment, exclusivity and termination provisions. Tools such as Luminance and Kira were built for this work. Findings still need lawyer review before they go into a report.

Do M&A lawyers need Harvey or Legora?

Not necessarily. Harvey and Legora are broad legal AI platforms that cover research, review and drafting across many practice areas, and they suit firms that want one tool for everything. A transactional team that mainly drafts and negotiates may get more from a tool scoped to that work.

Is it safe to put deal documents into legal AI?

It can be, if the vendor contractually commits not to train models on your data, keeps each firm's data separate, and answers your security questionnaire in writing. Check data location, sub-processors and retention before any live deal documents are uploaded.
