How better drafting helps law firms win more work

29 July 2026 · 3 min read · Jonny Braden

Winning new work is rarely about being the cheapest name on the panel. Clients choose the firm they trust to get a deal done cleanly and quickly. That means the things that win work are often the same things that make a firm good to deal with day to day. Turnaround. Confidence in the drafting. A sense that the firm has done this exact deal before.

Speed wins the first round

In a competitive pitch, the firm that turns a first draft around quickly makes a strong early impression. Clients read responsiveness as competence. When your team can produce a solid draft from the firm's precedents in hours rather than days, you look sharper than the firm still clearing its desk. Speed at the start of a relationship tends to be remembered.

Confidence keeps the client

Winning the work is one thing. Keeping the client is another, and it comes down to whether the output holds up. Drafting tools that draw on the firm's own precedents and flag inconsistencies help even a junior-heavy team put out work that reads like the firm's best. Fewer errors slip through. The client sees a consistent standard rather than a lottery based on who happened to be free that week.

Negotiation you can stand behind

The other half of winning work is how the deal is handled once it is live. A firm that can see, at a glance, the positions it has taken on similar deals before negotiates from a position of strength. It knows where it usually holds and where it usually concedes, and it does not leave that to whoever happens to remember the last matter. Clients notice when their lawyers negotiate with that kind of assurance, and they come back with the next deal.

The compounding effect

Put those together and the effect builds on itself. Faster turnaround wins the pitch. Consistent quality keeps the client. Sharper negotiation earns the referral. None of it requires a bigger team, only better tools behind the one you already have. That is the case for giving your lawyers something like Scriptum to work with.
