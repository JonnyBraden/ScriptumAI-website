What your tracked changes could be telling your firm

15 August 2026 · 3 min read

What your tracked changes tell your firm is more than a record of edits. Across dozens of transactions, they are a detailed negotiation history: which positions moved, which held, which clauses were consistently redrafted by the other side and which sailed through unchanged.

Tracked changes as a negotiation record

Every redline your firm produces and every counterparty markup it receives is a data point about how that negotiation moved. The deletion of a limitation of liability cap by the other side, the insertion of an extended warranty period, the complete replacement of an indemnity clause: each of these is recorded in the document itself, with author and timestamp, from the moment tracked changes is switched on.

Most firms treat this information as a working tool during the transaction and discard it when the deal closes. The accepted version is filed; the negotiation history is effectively lost. The pattern of how the deal moved, what the other side pushed for and what the firm ultimately agreed to is not preserved in a form that is useful for the next transaction.

What the patterns reveal

When tracked changes are analysed systematically across multiple transactions, patterns emerge that are not visible at the individual deal level. Certain clause types are consistently challenged by the other side across different counterparties, which may indicate that the firm's standard position is out of market. Particular associates tend to concede on specific issues faster than others, which is a training signal rather than a criticism. The firm's limitation of liability position survives some deal types reliably and gives way quickly in others, which is useful for pricing and negotiation preparation.

None of this analysis is available to a firm that treats each transaction as a standalone event.

Making the record useful

The practical challenge is that reading tracked changes manually across hundreds of documents is not feasible. The value of the data depends on being able to surface patterns without a manual review exercise that nobody has time for.
