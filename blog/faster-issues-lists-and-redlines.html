Producing issues lists and redlines in minutes, not hours

27 July 2026 · 3 min read · Jonny Braden

Faster issues lists and redlines are one of the most immediate ways AI-assisted drafting changes the economics of transactional work, and one of the areas where the gap between capable tools and average ones is most visible.

The time cost of an issues list

An issues list on a share purchase agreement or a development finance facility is not trivial to produce. It requires a careful read of the counterparty's draft, a comparison against your client's acceptable positions on each clause and a structured summary of what needs to move and why. For a mid-level associate, that might take a morning. For a junior, longer.

That time is recoverable on hourly billing, less so on fixed fees, and it delays the negotiation. Every hour between receiving a draft and issuing your issues list is an hour in which the deal is not moving. In a competitive mandate, responsiveness is a genuine differentiator.

What good AI assistance does here

A well-built legal AI tool can reduce the time to produce an initial issues list significantly, by comparing the counterparty draft against your firm's standard positions and flagging the deviations that need addressing. It does not replace the lawyer's judgment: the output still needs review and the commercial context still matters. But it gets a qualified lawyer from a blank page to a structured first pass far faster.

The same applies to redlines. Producing a first redline from your firm's precedent positions, inside the document the counterparty has provided, is a task that maps well to AI assistance once the tool understands your precedents.

Where the institutional knowledge matters

The difference between a tool drafting from generic training data and one drawing on your firm's own precedents is most visible in the issues list and redline. Generic output requires more review because it may not reflect your positions. Output grounded in your firm's actual negotiating history is closer to right from the start, which means the lawyer's review time goes on genuine edge cases rather than baseline corrections.
