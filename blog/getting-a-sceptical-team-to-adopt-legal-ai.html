Getting a sceptical team to adopt legal AI

15 August 2026 · 3 min read

Getting a sceptical team to adopt legal AI is a change management problem as much as a technology procurement one. The scepticism is usually reasonable: lawyers have seen legal technology investment cycles before, have experienced tools that promised transformation and delivered marginal improvements, and have learned to protect their time by waiting to see whether a new tool actually solves a problem they have before investing attention in learning it.

Understanding the specific objections

The first step is to understand what the scepticism is actually about. It is rarely a uniform rejection of AI. It is usually a specific combination of concerns: worry about the quality of AI output and whether it can be trusted, concern about the time required to learn a new tool and integrate it into an existing workflow, anxiety about what AI use means for professional development if associates are not doing the drafting work that builds their skills, and, sometimes, concern about what AI adoption means for the team's staffing levels.

Each of these is a legitimate concern and each requires a specific response. Addressing quality concerns requires showing the tool working on real document types rather than asserting that the quality is good. Addressing workflow concerns requires demonstrating that the tool integrates into existing processes rather than replacing them with something unfamiliar.

Starting with the right use cases

The adoption approach that works best is to start with the use cases that are most obviously painful for the team. If associates routinely spend time on high-volume, low-complexity documents that they find tedious, starting there demonstrates a benefit that the team will feel immediately. If partners are frustrated by inconsistency in associate output on a specific document type, starting there addresses a senior-level concern. Choosing the right entry point for a sceptical team matters more than starting with the most technically impressive feature.

Giving the team control

Adoption that is mandated from above without involving the team in the evaluation and configuration tends to produce surface compliance rather than genuine use. Giving the team input into how the tool is configured, which precedents are included and how the workflow is structured creates ownership that drives sustained adoption.
