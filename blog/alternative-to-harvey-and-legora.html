An alternative to Harvey and Legora for smaller firms

1 August 2026 · 3 min read · Jonny Braden

If you run a transactional practice with somewhere between twenty and a hundred fee earners, you have probably looked at Harvey or Legora and come away with the same feeling. Impressive tools, clearly built for someone else. Both are aimed at large firms with large budgets and the people to run a lengthy rollout. If that does not describe your firm, it is worth knowing what to look for instead.

Match the tool to the firm

Harvey made its name inside the biggest firms and in-house teams, with broad research and drafting features and pricing to match. Legora has planted its flag on multi-party collaboration. Both are genuinely capable products. The question is not whether they work, but whether a mid-sized firm will use enough of what they offer to justify the cost and the effort of adopting them. For a lot of firms the honest answer is no.

What a smaller firm should look for

A firm your size wants a few specific things. It wants a tool that lives where the lawyers already work, which for most of them means Microsoft Word rather than a separate portal they have to be talked into opening. It wants pricing it can read off a page, not a custom enterprise quote. And it wants something a small team can adopt in weeks, without a dedicated knowledge management function to keep it running.

The best tool for a hundred-lawyer firm and the best tool for a thousand-lawyer firm are rarely the same tool.

The thing most tools leave out

There is one more thing worth weighing, and it is easy to miss in a demo. Most legal AI helps you produce a document and stops there. It does not help the firm hold on to what it learns. For a smaller firm, where knowledge is concentrated in a handful of people, that is the feature that matters most. A tool that captures your precedents, positions and outcomes as your team works builds an asset the firm owns, rather than one you rent from a vendor.

Where Scriptum fits

Scriptum is built for exactly this gap. It works inside Word, publishes its pricing openly, and treats your firm's institutional knowledge as something to capture and keep, not just a means to a faster draft. If Harvey and Legora feel like more firm than yours needs, that is rather the point.
