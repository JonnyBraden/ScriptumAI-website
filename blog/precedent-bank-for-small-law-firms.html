How a small firm can build a precedent bank worth having

28 July 2026 · 3 min read

A precedent bank for small law firms sounds straightforward until you try to build one that people actually use. Most transactional practices have a folder somewhere, or a shared drive with historical documents, but the library that gets consulted before every first draft is rarer than the aspiration to have one.

What makes a precedent bank worth having

A precedent bank is only valuable if it stays current, reflects the firm's actual positions and is findable quickly under deal pressure. Historical documents that have not been reviewed for two years, filed under a naming convention nobody remembers creating, do not count. They are a liability as much as an asset: a lawyer who pulls a stale precedent and drafts from it without checking may be reproducing positions the firm has since moved away from.

The criteria for a useful precedent bank are simple even if meeting them is not. The documents should reflect the firm's current preferred positions. They should be organised so that the right precedent can be found in under two minutes. And they should update over time as the firm's practice evolves, without requiring a dedicated effort to keep them current.

The common failure modes

The most frequent reason precedent banks fail is that maintaining them competes with fee-earning work and loses. A partner resolves to review and update the precedents after a busy transaction period. The next transaction starts before that happens. The precedents drift further out of date. Eventually the team stops trusting the library and defaults to copying from recent completed deals instead, which is its own version of a precedent bank with worse curation.

The second failure mode is organisation. A library that captures everything but is hard to search is nearly as useless as no library at all.

A low-maintenance approach

The most practical path for a small firm is to treat the precedent bank as a byproduct of live work rather than a separate maintenance project. When a transaction closes, the final agreed versions of key documents become the updated precedent. The library reflects what the firm has actually agreed rather than what it hoped to agree. Curation happens at the deal level rather than as a separate administrative exercise.
