Fallback ladders: negotiating from a prepared position

15 August 2026 · 3 min read

Fallback ladders in contract negotiation give deal teams a structured sequence of positions to move through deliberately, rather than improvising concessions under pressure during a call or a late-night redline exchange.

What a fallback ladder is

A fallback ladder is a pre-agreed set of positions for a specific clause or deal point, ranked from ideal to acceptable, with each step defined in advance. Rather than opening with a position and then deciding in the moment how far to move if the other side pushes back, the deal team has already agreed among themselves which concessions are available, in which order, and at what point the position becomes a hard line.

The process of building the ladder is as valuable as the ladder itself. It forces the team to think through commercial priorities before the negotiation begins rather than during it. It also means that a junior lawyer handling a routine negotiation has the same prepared positions as the senior partner who built the original position.

Why improvising is risky

Without prepared fallback positions, concessions tend to happen for the wrong reasons. A counterparty pushes hard in the final hour before a signing deadline. The client is applying pressure to close. A junior associate, uncertain of the firm's acceptable range, concedes something that a more senior lawyer would have held. The result is a deal that closed, but on terms that were worse than they needed to be.

The damage accumulates across transactions. Individually, each concession looks like a reasonable response to commercial pressure. In aggregate, they represent a pattern of giving ground that a better-prepared team would not have given.

How to build and use one in practice

For each significant deal point, the preparation stage should include agreement on the opening position, the first fallback, the second fallback, and the walk-away condition. Each position should be accompanied by a brief rationale so that whoever is negotiating understands why it is the position, not just what it is.
