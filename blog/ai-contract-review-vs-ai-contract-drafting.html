AI contract review vs AI contract drafting: what is the difference?

Published 16 November 2026·5 min read
The short answer

AI contract review vs AI contract drafting comes down to direction. AI contract review reads a contract someone else has written and flags risks, gaps and departures from your playbook; AI contract drafting helps you write the document, or your response to a markup, from your own precedents. Most transactional firms spend more fee-earner time producing and turning drafts than reading third-party paper, so drafting is usually where the bigger saving sits.

Legal AI vendors increasingly offer both, so the labels on a product page can blur. Harvey, Legora, Spellbook, Luminance, Definely, Microsoft Copilot and Scriptum AI all touch contracts in some way. The useful question for a UK or Irish law firm, or an in-house legal team, is which job takes most of your team's week, and which kind of tool is built around that job.

What is the difference between AI contract review and AI contract drafting?

Review starts from a document you have received; drafting starts from a document you need to produce. That difference shapes what the tool needs to know and where its output lands.

 AI contract reviewAI contract drafting
Starting pointA contract or data room someone else preparedYour template, precedent or term sheet
Typical outputIssues list, risk flags, clause extraction, summaryA first draft, or tracked changes responding to a markup
What it must knowYour playbook and red linesYour precedents, preferred drafting and fallback positions
Where the time goes without itReading and summarisingAssembling, adapting and conforming drafts
Common fitDue diligence, third-party paper, high-volume intakeTransactional firms and in-house teams that hold the pen

A rule of thumb, not a ranking. Many tools do some of both.

What does AI contract review do well?

It is good at reading a lot of text quickly and consistently. On due diligence, a review tool can pull change-of-control, assignment and termination provisions out of hundreds of contracts so a lawyer can check the exceptions rather than read every page.

Luminance is particularly known for review, and Harvey and Legora both offer review across large document sets as part of a broader platform. For an in-house team that mainly receives suppliers' and customers' paper, a review-led tool checked against your playbook can take the first pass off a busy lawyer's desk.

What does AI contract drafting do well?

It shortens the slowest part of transactional work: getting from a blank page, or an old deal, to a sound first draft and then turning each markup. Done well, it starts from the firm's own precedents rather than generic clause text, so the draft reads like your firm wrote it.

Where it runs matters. Lawyers draft and negotiate in Microsoft Word, so a drafting tool that works as a Word add-in and returns tracked changes fits the way the work is already done. Our guide to the best AI tool for drafting contracts in Microsoft Word covers what to look for, and what AI-assisted contract drafting is explains the basics.

Review tells you what is wrong with someone else's draft; drafting decides how good your own draft is in the first place.

Which should a law firm or in-house team buy first?

Buy for the task that consumes the most qualified time. Work through these questions with the lawyers who will use the tool.

  1. In a typical month, do we produce more first drafts, or receive more third-party paper?
  2. How much of our time goes on turning markups compared with reading incoming documents?
  3. Do we run large due diligence exercises often enough to justify a dedicated review tool?
  4. Can the tool work from our own precedents and playbook, and show where a suggestion came from?
  5. Does the output land in Word as tracked changes, or in a separate portal we must copy from?
  6. Who owns the clause positions and knowledge the tool captures, and can we export them?

If most answers point to drafting and markups, start there, and use review features as a second step. A firm doing heavy disputes or diligence work may reasonably choose the other way round. Our guide to reviewing a counterparty's markup with AI covers the overlap between the two.

Can one legal AI tool do both review and drafting?

Yes, most can do both to some degree, so judge them on emphasis. Harvey and Legora are broad legal platforms that cover research, review and drafting, and Legora now markets to small and mid-sized firms as well as large ones. Spellbook and Definely are Word add-ins used for drafting and review. Microsoft Copilot offers general writing help across Microsoft 365. In our view, a transactional firm paying for a broad platform can end up funding capability built for litigation-heavy use that it rarely opens.

Common questions

What is the difference between AI contract review and AI contract drafting?

AI contract review reads a contract someone else has written and flags risks, missing terms and departures from your playbook. AI contract drafting helps you write the first draft, or your response to a markup, usually from your own templates and precedents.

Is AI contract review or AI contract drafting more useful for a law firm?

For a transactional firm where most fee-earner time goes into producing first drafts and turning markups, drafting usually saves more time. For teams that mainly receive third-party paper or run large due diligence exercises, review is often the better first purchase.

Can one legal AI tool do both review and drafting?

Yes. Most current tools, including Harvey, Legora, Spellbook and Scriptum AI, offer both to some degree. The difference is emphasis, so test each tool on the task that takes most of your team's time rather than relying on the feature list.

Does AI contract drafting replace a lawyer's judgement?

No. A drafting tool produces a starting point. A qualified lawyer still decides the positions, checks the output and approves anything before it goes to a client or the other side, in line with the SRA's expectations on competence and supervision.

What should we test in a trial of an AI contract tool?

Run it on a live matter: one first draft from your own template and one counterparty markup. Check whether the output follows your precedents, whether changes land as tracked changes in Word, and whether you can see where each suggestion came from.
