Capturing deal knowledge before a lawyer leaves

24 July 2026 · 3 min read

Capturing deal knowledge before a lawyer leaves is a problem every transactional firm faces, and most handle it badly. Not through negligence, but because the tools and processes that would make it work have not been built into the way lawyers do their daily work.

What actually leaves with a departing lawyer

The visible loss when a senior associate or partner departs is the client relationship and the pipeline of work they may take with them. The less visible loss is the accumulated deal knowledge that was never written down: which clause positions the firm holds across different deal types, which counterparties they have faced before and how those parties negotiate, what drove particular commercial decisions on transactions over the past five years, which standard positions the firm agreed to move away from and why.

None of this appears on a file. It exists in the lawyer's memory and in their individual drafting habits, and it walks out the door with them.

Why exit interviews do not solve it

The standard response is an exit interview. A partner sits down with the departing lawyer and asks them to describe what they know. This produces a conversation, occasionally a document, usually filed in an inbox and not retrieved again. The information captured is incomplete, compressed by memory and not structured in a form that is useful to the next lawyer who handles a similar transaction.

The deeper problem is that exit interviews capture knowledge after it has already left the active work of the firm. By the time the lawyer is in their notice period, the opportunity to integrate what they know into the firm's ongoing practice has already passed.

What systematic capture looks like

The approach that works is to capture deal knowledge during the transaction rather than after it. When a lawyer makes a clause choice, takes a position in a negotiation, or records why a deal closed on particular terms, that information enters the firm's knowledge base immediately and is available to whoever handles the next similar deal. The departing lawyer's knowledge has already been embedded in the system long before any exit conversation takes place.
