Why the quality of the first draft decides everything

15 August 2026 · 3 min read

Why the first draft decides so much in contract negotiation is not a mystery to any experienced transactional lawyer. The party that produces the initial draft chooses the structure, sets the starting positions and, largely, determines which issues the other side will spend its energy on.

Who controls the frame

The drafter's advantage in a commercial negotiation is structural. You choose which issues to address explicitly and which to leave to implication. You choose how completion mechanics are expressed, which warranties are included and how they are scoped, which conditions appear and how they are drafted. The other side's job is to identify what they want to change, which means their effort is organised around your document rather than around their preferred outcome.

A well-drafted first document does not guarantee a good deal result. But a poor first document requires the drafter's own client to accept a worse starting position, because the other side will be working from a structure that was not designed with that client's interests as the priority.

The anchoring effect

Negotiation research consistently shows that the first number or position introduced in a negotiation has an outsized influence on where the final position lands. The same applies to the first contract draft. A limitation of liability cap set at one times the contract value in the opening draft is the point the parties negotiate from, even if the drafter's ideal outcome is two times. Getting a first draft out quickly, with the right positions clearly articulated, is partly a negotiation tactic as well as a service standard.

Speed and quality together

The tension is that quality first drafts take time, and time to first draft is itself a competitive factor. A client who needs a share purchase agreement produced before a competing bidder's advisers submit theirs is asking for speed and quality simultaneously. A firm that can produce a well-grounded, comprehensive first draft in two days rather than five is in a materially better position to serve that client.

AI-assisted drafting from firm precedents changes this calculation. The structural and boilerplate work that consumes a significant fraction of first-draft time is produced quickly from established positions, and the lawyer's attention is directed to the deal-specific judgment calls that actually determine quality.
