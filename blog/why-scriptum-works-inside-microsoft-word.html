Why Scriptum works inside Microsoft Word, not a separate portal

4 August 2026 · 3 min read

Why Scriptum works inside Microsoft Word rather than a separate portal is a deliberate choice, and it matters more to day-to-day adoption than it might first appear.

Where lawyers actually work

A transactional lawyer's working day is organised around Word. First drafts, redlines, tracked changes, comments, version management, the final clean version for execution: all of it happens in a Word document. The discipline of the document is the discipline of the transaction.

Many AI drafting tools ask lawyers to step outside that environment and use a separate portal instead. The portal may have a clean interface and good features, but it is not where the work lives. Content has to be pasted in, worked on, and pasted back out again. Tracked changes may not survive the round trip. Comments get lost. The document that leaves the AI system is not necessarily the same document that has been circulating between parties.

Why switching applications reduces adoption

Adoption in a professional services firm depends on the tool fitting the existing workflow, not the other way around. When AI assistance is a click away inside Word, lawyers use it. When it requires opening a second application, logging into a separate system and copying text across, most lawyers do not bother, particularly under deadline pressure.

This is not a failure of will. It is a rational response to friction. The legal AI tool that gets used consistently is the one that demands the least change to established habits. A tool that gets used by ten percent of your team, ten percent of the time, produces ten percent of the value the subscription cost.

What staying in Word makes possible

There are practical advantages beyond adoption rates. Staying inside Word means tracked changes work as they always have. Comments appear in the right place. Version history is managed through your existing document management system. Nothing needs reformatting before it can be used in a negotiation.

It also means that the context available to Scriptum includes the actual document in progress: the structure, the defined terms, the clauses already agreed, the positions the other side has taken. A drafting assistant that can see the whole document produces better suggestions than one working from a snippet pasted into a separate interface.

A simpler path to rollout

For a firm with 20 to 100 fee earners, a tool that lives inside Word removes the need for a change management programme. You do not have to persuade your team to log into something new every day or build a habit around a separate product. You install an add-in, and the capability is available whenever they need it, inside the tool they already have open.
