How law firms can beat the legal talent crisis

18 July 2026 · 3 min read · Jonny Braden

Every managing partner is having the same two conversations this year. One is about how hard it has become to hire good transactional lawyers. The other is about how to keep the ones they already have. The legal talent crisis is not just a problem for the large firms in the City. It lands hardest on firms with twenty to a hundred fee earners, where a single departure can leave a whole practice area short.

Hiring your way out is getting harder and more expensive. Salaries have climbed, good candidates have their pick of offers, and it can take months to bring someone up to speed once they arrive. The work does not slow down while you wait. Clients still expect their share purchase agreement back by Friday.

The real cost of being short-staffed

When a firm is short of people, the same amount of work lands on fewer shoulders. Junior lawyers draft from scratch because nobody is free to point them at the right precedent. Senior lawyers spend their evenings reviewing instead of winning new work. Turnaround slips, quality wobbles, and the people you most want to keep start to feel the strain. That is how a hiring problem quietly turns into a retention problem.

Getting more from the team you have

The firms coping best are not the ones hiring fastest. They are the ones getting more out of every lawyer already on the payroll. AI-assisted drafting is a large part of that. When a tool produces a solid first draft from the firm's own precedents, a junior lawyer starts from something good rather than a blank page. Review gets lighter. A team of six begins to handle the workload of eight, and nobody has to work later to do it.

There is a retention benefit too. Lawyers rarely leave because they are busy. They leave because too much of their day is spent on work that feels mechanical and thankless. Take the drudgery out of the first draft and you give people more time on the parts of the job they trained for and actually enjoy.

A quiet fix, not a grand plan

You will not solve the talent crisis with a recruitment drive alone. You solve it by making each lawyer's day more productive and less of a grind, so the work still gets done and good people have a reason to stay. That is the practical case for putting drafting tools like Scriptum in front of your team.
