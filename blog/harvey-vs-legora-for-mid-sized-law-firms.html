Harvey vs Legora: which is better for a mid-sized law firm?

Published 24 November 2026·6 min read
The short answer

Harvey vs Legora is a close call for a mid-sized law firm: both are well-funded, broad legal AI platforms that cover research, document review and drafting across practice areas, and both now market to mid-sized and boutique firms and in-house teams as well as the largest law firms. Neither publishes seat prices. For a transactional practice, the better question is often whether you need a broad platform at all.

Harvey and Legora are the two names most mid-sized firms hear first when they start looking at legal AI, and many shortlist both. This comparison sets out what each company says about itself, where they differ, and how a corporate or commercial team at a firm in the UK, Ireland, the USA or Canada might choose between them, or look elsewhere. We build a competing product, Scriptum AI, so we have tried to stick to what each vendor publishes.

What is the difference between Harvey and Legora?

The products overlap more than they differ. Both offer an AI assistant, document review and drafting, with agentic workflows on top; the clearer differences are in origin, customer base and how each positions itself.

Is Harvey or Legora better for a mid-sized law firm?

Neither wins outright; the answer depends on what your fee earners spend their week doing. If the firm wants one platform for litigation, research and corporate work, either is a credible choice and a side-by-side pilot is the only fair test.

Both now sell to firms of 50 or 200 fee earners explicitly, so size alone does not settle it. Both also have large-firm adoption: Harvey cites 80% of the Am Law 100, and in the UK Addleshaw Goddard and Kennedys have announced firmwide arrangements; Legora cites more than half of the AmLaw 50. Big-firm references tell you the product works at scale; they do not tell you what a 60-lawyer firm will pay or how much support it will get.

How should a mid-sized firm compare Harvey and Legora?

Compare them on your own documents and your own commercial terms, not on funding headlines. Use this checklist in both pilots:

  1. Pilot on live-style work. Give both the same anonymised SPA, facility agreement or supply contract and the same tasks.
  2. Test inside Microsoft Word. Transactional lawyers draft and negotiate in Word with tracked changes. See how each handles an incoming markup, not just a blank-page draft.
  3. Check the drafts against your precedents. Ask whether output starts from your firm's agreed positions or from generic market language.
  4. Get the price in writing. Neither publishes seat prices, so ask for the per-seat cost, minimum seats, term and any usage caps.
  5. Ask who owns what the tool learns. Clause positions and playbooks built during use should belong to the firm.
  6. Settle security before uploading client data. Get model training, data separation, location and retention commitments into the contract.
The fair test is not which platform does more, but which one your lawyers will still be using in month six.

Does a transactional team need either platform?

Not always. A broad platform earns its cost when the firm uses its breadth; a corporate or commercial team whose day is drafting and markup rounds may use only part of it.

It is Scriptum's view that on broad per-seat platforms, transactional users effectively subsidise the heavier usage of litigation work, which consumes far more processing on large document sets. Key takeaways

  • Harvey and Legora are both capable, broad legal AI platforms; the product overlap is large.
  • Both now market to mid-sized firms, so the choice comes down to fit, price and support rather than firm size.
  • Neither publishes seat prices, so get commercial terms in writing early.
  • Pilot both on your own documents, in Word, before committing.
  • A transactional-only team should check whether it will use the breadth it is paying for.

Common questions

Is Harvey or Legora better for a mid-sized law firm?

Neither is better for every firm. Both are broad legal AI platforms covering research, review and drafting. Both now market to mid-sized firms as well as the largest firms and in-house teams. Run both against your own documents before deciding, and consider whether a narrower tool fits a transactional practice better.

How much do Harvey and Legora cost?

At the time of writing, neither Harvey nor Legora publishes seat prices on its website, so pricing is agreed with each vendor. Ask for the price per seat, the minimum number of seats, the contract term and any usage limits in writing.

Are there alternatives to Harvey and Legora for transactional lawyers?

Yes. Drafting tools that work inside Microsoft Word, such as Spellbook, Definely and Scriptum AI, focus on drafting and negotiating agreements rather than covering every practice area. Microsoft Copilot is a general assistant rather than a legal-specific tool.

Can in-house legal teams use Harvey or Legora?

Yes. Both sell to in-house teams. Harvey says five of the Fortune 10 use it, and Legora reported that in-house teams made up more than 40% of its new customers in the third quarter of 2026.
