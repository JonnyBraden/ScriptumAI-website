Keeping your best senior lawyers in a tight market

16 July 2026 · 3 min read

Keeping senior lawyers in a tight market is increasingly not a compensation story alone. Pay matters, but experienced transactional lawyers who are evaluating their options are also looking at the quality of the work environment and whether the firm gives them the infrastructure to do their best work without unnecessary friction.

What senior lawyers want beyond compensation

A senior associate or counsel who has spent eight years building a transactional practice has options. They can move laterally to a firm that pays more, join an in-house team, or go independent. What keeps them at a mid-market firm, where the brand recognition is lower and the pay ceiling is not at the top of the market, is usually a combination of autonomy, quality of colleagues and the sense that the work itself is interesting and well-resourced.

Quality of tooling matters in this mix. A senior lawyer who spends significant time on administrative drafting work that better tools would eliminate is a lawyer who is conscious, every day, of what they could be doing instead.

The leverage problem

One of the strongest retention factors for a senior lawyer is having leverage: good junior support, clear processes that allow them to run deals efficiently, and infrastructure that multiplies their output rather than constraining it. A senior lawyer who is well-leveraged can handle more work, develop junior lawyers more effectively and deliver better results for clients. That experience is satisfying in a way that being the bottleneck in a poorly resourced team is not.

Firms that invest in giving their senior lawyers better leverage are investing in retention, whether or not they frame it that way.

How better tools change the conversation

AI-assisted drafting changes the leverage calculation directly. A senior lawyer working with Scriptum can review and approve a solid first draft rather than producing it from scratch, can brief a junior on a deal with access to structured precedent and position history rather than a verbal download, and can turn around counterparty documents faster because the initial analysis is done. The work remains interesting, the judgment requirement remains high, and the mechanical overhead is reduced.
