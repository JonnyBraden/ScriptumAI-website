Why responsiveness wins more legal work than reputation

6 July 2026 · 3 min read

Why responsiveness wins more legal work than reputation alone is something most transactional firms discover when they lose an instruction to a firm they consider their inferior on quality grounds. The client chose the other firm because they picked up the phone faster and turned around the engagement letter in two hours.

What the turnaround expectation looks like now

Client expectations around response times have compressed considerably. A corporate client who needs an NDA drafted for an imminent process expects a response and a first cut on the same day. A private equity house running a tight acquisition timetable expects its advisers to match the pace of the process. A founder who has engaged two firms simultaneously will go with whichever responds first with a coherent engagement proposal.

Reputation gets you on the list. Responsiveness determines whether you get the instruction. A firm known for excellent work that consistently delivers three days after competitors deliver one day is not trading on its reputation for long.

What responsiveness signals to clients

Fast turnaround signals more than operational efficiency. It signals that your team has capacity, which means the client's matter will not queue behind other work. It signals that your processes are organised, which suggests the whole transaction will be handled smoothly. It signals that your team is prepared, which means they are not starting from scratch every time.

Slow turnaround signals the opposite of all three, regardless of the quality of the eventual output. A client who waited four days for an NDA will remember that, even if the document was very good when it arrived.

How drafting speed connects to winning work

The firms that are consistently fastest at first drafts tend to have the best-organised precedent bases and the clearest internal processes. They are not working harder; they have removed the reconstruction time that slower firms spend on each new document. AI-assisted drafting from firm precedents extends this advantage to teams that have not previously had the infrastructure to compete on speed.
