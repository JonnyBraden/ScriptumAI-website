Cutting NDA turnaround from days to minutes

14 July 2026 · 3 min read

Cutting NDA turnaround from days to minutes is one of the clearest illustrations of what AI-assisted drafting can do for a transactional practice. NDAs are the most common document a transactional lawyer handles and, relative to their complexity, one of the slowest to produce.

Why NDAs take longer than they should

The standard NDA is not a difficult document. It has a well-established structure, a limited range of commercially significant issues and positions that most experienced transactional lawyers could agree in a short call. Despite this, a firm that drafts its NDA from a precedent that needs customisation, reviews the other side's form from scratch each time, or lacks a clear view of which positions it will hold, tends to treat each NDA as a small but meaningful piece of work that competes with everything else on the associate's desk.

The result is that NDAs queue. A client who needs one to start a due diligence process waits two days. An opportunity is delayed. In a market where responsiveness is a competitive factor, the ability to turn around an NDA in under an hour is a genuine differentiator.

What faster looks like in practice

A firm with a well-configured AI drafting tool can produce its standard NDA in minutes from a set of deal parameters: mutual or unilateral, duration, scope of permitted use, carve-outs for existing knowledge. If the other side's form comes in, the initial issues analysis against the firm's standard positions is generated quickly, and the lawyer's job becomes reviewing that analysis rather than producing it.

The redline or response letter that follows can be similarly accelerated. The positions are already defined; the drafting reflects the firm's established language rather than being produced fresh each time.

Quality at speed

The concern with acceleration is quality. An NDA that gets the firm's positions wrong is worse than a slow one. The quality ceiling with AI assistance is set by the quality of the precedents the tool is trained on. When Scriptum drafts from the firm's own NDAs, the output reflects the language the firm has already approved and used in practice, not a generic alternative that may not reflect how the firm actually approaches confidentiality obligations.
