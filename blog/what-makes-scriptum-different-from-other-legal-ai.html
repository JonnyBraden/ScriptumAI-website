What makes Scriptum different from other legal AI tools

26 July 2026 · 3 min read

When legal AI tools started appearing in transactional practices, most of them made a similar promise: faster drafts, less repetitive work. That promise has largely held. What it has not always addressed is where the drafting happens, what knowledge it draws on, and what your firm is left with once the matter closes. Those three gaps are where Scriptum is built differently.

It works where lawyers already work

Most legal AI tools live in a browser portal. Lawyers move in and out of Word, copying text or switching between systems, which adds friction and breaks concentration. Scriptum runs directly inside Microsoft Word as an add-in. The drafting environment does not change. There is no new interface to learn, no context switching, no export step. For a firm trying to get its team to actually use a tool day to day, that matters considerably.

The adoption question for any new legal tech is almost always the same: will lawyers actually open it? If the answer depends on forming a new habit of visiting a separate portal, the honest answer for most fee earners is no, at least not consistently. A tool that lives inside Word removes that barrier at source.

It drafts from your precedents, not generic text

General-purpose AI assistants are trained on publicly available text. Their output reflects what contracts typically look like, not what your firm specifically does. Scriptum drafts from the firm's own precedent bank. When an associate generates a first draft, it reflects the positions your partners have taken, the clauses your team prefers, and the language your clients have already accepted. Quality is not averaged down to the market median; it is calibrated to your standards.

It builds something your firm keeps

This is the most distinctive part. Other legal AI tools process your work and produce an output. Scriptum, as a byproduct of normal drafting and negotiation activity, builds a structured record: clause choices, negotiation positions, counterparty behaviour, deal outcomes. That record belongs to your firm. Over time it compounds, improving every draft and every negotiation that follows. When a senior lawyer leaves, their accumulated knowledge does not leave with them in the same way it once did.

Pricing you can model before the first call

Enterprise legal AI can involve lengthy sales cycles and pricing that only appears after several conversations. Scriptum publishes its founding-firm pricing: 69 pounds per user per month. For a firm of 20 to 100 fee earners, that is a number you can put into a spreadsheet and bring to a partners' meeting the same afternoon. No procurement function required.
