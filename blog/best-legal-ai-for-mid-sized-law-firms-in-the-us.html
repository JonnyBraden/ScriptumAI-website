What is the best legal AI for mid-sized law firms in the US?

Published 25 November 2026·6 min read
The short answer

The best legal AI for a mid-sized law firm in the US depends on the work it does. For a firm whose fee income comes mainly from transactional work, the best fit is usually a tool inside Microsoft Word that drafts from the firm's own precedents, handles the other side's markup and builds knowledge the firm owns. Broad platforms such as Harvey and Legora suit firms that also need research and litigation support in the same tool.

US mid-sized firms now have more choice in legal AI than ever. Harvey, Legora, Spellbook, Microsoft Copilot and Scriptum AI all sell to firms of this size, and most of them will offer a demo within the week. This guide is for corporate, commercial and real estate partners at firms of roughly 5 to 500 lawyers, and for in-house teams buying alongside them. It sets out how to choose, what the ABA expects of you, and where each type of tool fits.

What should a mid-sized US firm look for in legal AI?

Look for fit with the work your lawyers actually bill, not the longest feature list. A transactional practice spends its hours on first drafts and markup rounds, so the tool has to be good at both.

  1. Does it work inside Microsoft Word? Agreements are drafted and negotiated in Word with tracked changes. A tool that sits in a separate browser tab will be skipped when a deal is busy.
  2. Does it draft from your precedents? Your forms reflect years of agreed positions. Drafts should start there, not from generic language.
  3. Can it handle incoming markup? Ask to see a real redline turned round, with an issues list, not a single clause rewritten.
  4. Does a lawyer approve everything? Nothing should reach the client or the other side without sign-off.
  5. Who owns the knowledge? Positions agreed on each matter should build a clause library that stays with the firm.
  6. Is the price published? You should be able to model the cost per lawyer before a sales call.
  7. Are the security answers in writing? Model training, data location, sub-processors and retention belong in the contract.

What does ABA Formal Opinion 512 mean for buying legal AI?

It means the buying decision is part of your ethics compliance. ABA Formal Opinion 512, issued on 29 July 2024, confirms lawyers may use generative AI but must still meet their duties of competence, confidentiality, client communication, supervision and reasonable fees.

In practice, that turns into questions for every vendor. Can your lawyers understand and check what the tool produces? Is client information kept confidential and out of model training? Can partners supervise what associates and agents do? And if the tool saves time, does your billing reflect it? State bars have issued their own guidance too, so check the rules where you practise. Common questions

Is legal AI allowed under the ABA Model Rules?

Yes. ABA Formal Opinion 512, issued in July 2024, says lawyers may use generative AI tools but must meet their existing duties of competence, confidentiality, communication, supervision and reasonable fees. State bar rules and guidance also apply, so check your own jurisdiction.

Do mid-sized US firms need Harvey?

Not necessarily. Harvey is a capable broad platform and has a dedicated offer for mid-sized and boutique firms. It suits firms that want research, litigation support and transactional work in one tool. A firm whose work is mainly deals may get more from a tool built only for drafting and negotiation.

Can a US law firm use a legal AI tool built in the UK?

Yes, provided the tool handles US documents and drafting conventions, and its data handling meets your clients' requirements. Ask where data is stored, whether it is used to train models and who the sub-processors are, and get the answers in writing.

Does Scriptum AI work for US law firms?

Yes. Scriptum AI is built for transactional work at mid-sized firms with between 5 and 500 fee earners in the UK, Ireland, the USA and Canada. It runs inside Microsoft Word, drafts from the firm's own precedents and publishes its pricing, including in US dollars.

How should a mid-sized firm trial legal AI?

Run a short pilot on real, closed matters with a small group of lawyers who do the work every day. Score each tool on first-draft quality, how it handles incoming markup, how much checking it needs and whether lawyers keep using it after the first fortnight.
