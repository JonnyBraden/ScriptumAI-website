Ending the defined-terms headache in long contracts

15 August 2026 · 3 min read

Ending the defined-terms headache in long contracts is one of the least glamorous but most practically valuable things AI-assisted drafting can offer a transactional team. Defined terms inconsistencies are a perennial source of last-minute corrections, associate embarrassment and, occasionally, genuine legal uncertainty.

Why defined terms cause problems

The problem is structural. In a long contract, defined terms are used across dozens of clauses, sometimes hundreds of pages apart. When a clause is added late in the drafting process, a new term may be introduced without being listed in the definitions section. When a term from the counterparty's draft is incorporated without renaming, two terms may refer to the same concept. When the document has been through multiple rounds of negotiation with tracked changes and several lawyers contributing, the consistency of capitalisation and reference can break down in ways that are easy to miss on a final read.

None of these errors is difficult to make. In a document of 60 or 80 pages under time pressure, they are actually quite hard to avoid through manual review alone.

The consistency problem at scale

The difficulty compounds when a firm is producing multiple versions of a long-form document across a deal with several parties, or when a single document goes through extensive revision over weeks of negotiation. Each round of changes introduces the risk of new inconsistencies. An associate who catches every defined terms issue in a fifty-page document is doing genuinely skilled review work; expecting that level of consistency across five rounds of a complex SPA is expecting a lot.

The cost is not just reputational. A defined term that is used without definition, or a term that is defined but inconsistently applied, can become a point of dispute during the transaction or after it closes.

A better approach

AI-assisted drafting that is aware of the full document context can identify defined terms issues as they arise rather than after the fact. When a new clause is drafted, the system checks whether the terms used are defined. When the counterparty's language is incorporated, existing terms are flagged. The defined terms schedule stays consistent with the body of the document because the tool is tracking both simultaneously.
