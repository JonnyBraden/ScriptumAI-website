How to evaluate a legal AI drafting tool: a checklist

3 August 2026 · 3 min read · Jonny Braden

Evaluating a legal AI drafting tool requires asking sharper questions than most vendors expect. The market is busy and the demos are polished. Here is how to cut through.

Does it work where you already work?

Start here. If the tool requires lawyers to leave Microsoft Word and log into a separate portal, ask for evidence that your team will actually use it six months after rollout. Adoption is the only metric that matters, and friction kills adoption faster than almost anything else. The best legal AI drafting tools work inside the applications lawyers already use, not alongside them. A tool that requires a context switch to use will be used selectively at first and not at all by month four.

What is it trained on?

This is the most important technical question. A tool trained on publicly available text will produce generic legal language. A tool trained on your firm's own precedents will produce output that reflects your house style, your negotiated positions, and your commercial judgement. Ask the vendor directly: when I draft a clause, what is the model drawing on? If the answer is anything other than your own documents, understand clearly what that means for output quality and how much review work falls to your lawyers.

Does knowledge accumulate over time?

One-shot AI tools that produce output and forget are useful for individual tasks but do not build firm value over time. The tools worth investing in capture the outputs of each matter, the positions taken in each negotiation, and the clauses that worked, creating an institutional knowledge base that improves with use. Ask how the tool handles knowledge accumulation and, critically, who owns that knowledge. It should belong to your firm, not the vendor.

Is the pricing transparent?

Opaque enterprise pricing is a signal. It usually means the tool is designed and priced for large firms with large budgets, and that smaller firms pay a premium for the privilege of not knowing what a fair price looks like. Per-user transparent pricing published on the website means you can assess the commercial case without a sales cycle. If the vendor cannot tell you the price in a first conversation, that is information worth having.

How long does onboarding take?

For a firm of 20 to 100 fee earners without a dedicated IT function or knowledge management team, a long implementation project is a real cost. Ask for a realistic onboarding timeline and what your firm is expected to contribute to it. If the answer involves months of configuration or a significant data migration exercise, make sure you have budgeted for all of it before you sign.

What happens to your data?

Client confidentiality is not negotiable. Before any AI tool touches your documents, establish exactly where your data goes, whether it is used to train the vendor's shared model, and what controls exist over access. This is a professional obligation, not a nice-to-have, and a vendor who cannot answer clearly is one to avoid.
