Legal AI that builds knowledge your firm owns

26 July 2026 · 3 min read

Legal AI that builds knowledge your firm owns is not a common category. Most tools work the other way: they take your firm's work as input and return only the immediate output. The institutional knowledge embedded in your drafts, the positions you took in negotiations, the clauses you fought for or conceded, none of that is captured anywhere useful. It moves through each matter and disappears. Scriptum is built to address exactly that.

What institutional knowledge actually means

Institutional knowledge is not the documents a firm produces. It is the reasoning behind them. Why did your team accept that particular indemnity cap? Which counterparty consistently pushes back on governing law? What fallback position did you settle on in the last three share purchase agreement negotiations? That knowledge lives in senior lawyers' heads. When they leave, it leaves with them.

Most firms recognise this as a problem. Few have a practical way to address it, because capturing knowledge formally has historically required a dedicated knowledge management function that a firm of 20 to 100 fee earners cannot justify staffing.

How Scriptum captures it without extra work

Scriptum does not ask lawyers to log their decisions into a separate system. As a normal part of drafting and negotiating inside Microsoft Word, it records what was chosen and why. Clause selections, redline outcomes, accepted and rejected positions. The records accumulate as a byproduct of work that was happening anyway. No KM team required. No forms to complete between calls.

The result is a structured record of how your firm actually operates. Each new matter draws on everything that came before it. A junior lawyer producing a first draft gets the benefit of positions your partners took years to develop and refine.

Why firm ownership matters more than it might seem

With many tools, your data trains the vendor's model. What you put in improves a shared system that your competitors also use. Scriptum's knowledge base belongs to your firm and is not used to train anything beyond your own environment. It is yours to build on, yours to keep, and yours to take with you if you ever leave.

The value of that compounds. A firm that has been using Scriptum for two years has a knowledge asset that a competitor cannot replicate quickly. Not because the technology is inaccessible, but because the firm-specific record of positions, precedents and outcomes takes time to build. That is a genuine competitive advantage, generated as a side effect of doing normal legal work.
