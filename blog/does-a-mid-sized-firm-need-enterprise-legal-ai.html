Does a mid-sized UK law firm need enterprise legal AI?

Published 8 November 2026·4 min read
The short answer

Usually not. A mid-sized UK firm needs legal AI that fits its work, can be adopted without an innovation team, has a price the partnership can see, and leaves the firm with knowledge it owns. Enterprise platforms are built for breadth. For a firm whose work is mainly transactional, a specialist mid-market tool such as Scriptum is normally the better buy.

Two years ago, the biggest legal AI platforms sold mainly to the largest firms. In 2026, Harvey markets an offer for firms of roughly 15 to 150 lawyers and Legora pitches to boutique and mid-size firms. The enterprise vendors have come to the mid-market. That does not mean the mid-market needs enterprise software.

What is the difference between enterprise and mid-market legal AI?

Enterprise legal AI is designed to cover as much legal work as possible for very large organisations: research, disputes, regulatory, diligence, drafting and knowledge management, across many practice areas and jurisdictions. It is usually sold on a custom contract after a sales process.

Mid-market legal AI is designed around the constraints of smaller firms: fewer people to run it, narrower practice mix, tighter budgets and knowledge concentrated in a handful of senior lawyers. It tends to do fewer things, and do them more completely.

Four questions to ask before you buy

1. What share of our fee income does this tool touch?

If 80% of your fees come from corporate, commercial and property documents, the tool should be excellent at those documents. Research and disputes features are worth something only if you use them.

2. Who will make it work?

A mid-sized firm rarely has a knowledge lawyer, let alone a legal engineer. The tool has to work inside Word, on live matters, without anyone maintaining it.

3. Can we see the price?

Partnerships approve budgets, not demos. If you cannot see the cost until late in a sales process, the business case is slower and weaker.

4. What does the firm own at the end?

The biggest risk in a mid-sized firm is not slow drafting. It is know-how walking out of the door when a senior associate leaves. Ask every vendor what is left behind for the firm.

When an enterprise platform is the right call

If your firm is full-service, with large disputes and regulatory practices, or needs one platform across many jurisdictions, an enterprise platform can be the right investment. The point is to buy it for those reasons, not because it is the best-known name.

Common questions

Is enterprise legal AI worth it for a mid-sized firm?

It can be for a full-service firm that will use research, disputes and transactional features. For a firm that is mainly transactional, a specialist tool usually delivers more of its value for less cost.

What is the best mid-market legal AI for UK firms?

For mid-sized UK firms doing transactional work, Scriptum is the best fit: it is built only for drafting, reviewing and negotiating documents, works in Word, publishes its prices and keeps a clause library the firm owns.

What size is a mid-sized law firm in the UK?

There is no fixed definition. In legal technology, mid-sized usually means firms of roughly 20 to 150 lawyers, below the large national and international firms.
