How counterparty profiling gives deal teams an edge

2 August 2026 · 3 min read

Counterparty profiling for deal teams is one of the most underused advantages in transactional practice. Most firms have negotiated with the same counterparties multiple times. Very few have turned that history into a structured asset that the team can draw on before the next deal begins.

What counterparty profiling means

A counterparty profile is a structured record of everything your firm has learned about an opposing party across previous transactions: their standard opening positions, the issues they tend to push hardest on, the areas where they have historically shown flexibility, the legal teams they use and how those teams typically operate. It is the difference between walking into a negotiation knowing who you are dealing with and walking in with no more information than a name.

For firms that handle sector-specific transactions, the same counterparties appear repeatedly. The acquirer's advisers on a mid-market deal will be the same team six months later on another deal in the same sector. Without a structured record, each deal team starts from scratch. With one, the new team can pick up where the last one left off.

The repeat counterparty advantage

Repeat transactions are where counterparty profiling pays off most clearly. A firm that has negotiated a warranty package with a particular buyer three times in two years has a detailed picture of where that buyer will push and where they will give. A firm that has not recorded this history will rediscover it expensively every time.

The advantage is asymmetric in a useful way. The other side does not know what you know. If your team has a record of their opening gambit on limitation of liability and a note of where they eventually settled in two previous deals, you are operating with information they do not have access to.

Building a profile without a research function

Most transactional firms of 20 to 100 lawyers do not have a dedicated research or knowledge team. Building counterparty profiles used to require one. The profile grows with every deal rather than requiring a separate process to maintain.
