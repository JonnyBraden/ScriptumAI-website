The client experience edge in transactional law

1 July 2026 · 3 min read

The client experience edge in transactional law is not built on technical quality alone. Most clients who switch firms do not leave because the legal advice was poor. They leave because the communication was inconsistent, the turnaround times were unpredictable or the experience of working with the team was frustrating in ways that had nothing to do with the legal substance.

What clients actually notice

In a transaction, the client's direct experience of the firm is shaped by how quickly the firm responds, how clearly it communicates, how well the team co-ordinates internally and how smoothly the document process runs. A client who receives the first draft two hours after requesting it, who can track the current version of the key document easily, and who is never chasing the firm for an update has a qualitatively different experience from one who waits, chases and receives documents with inconsistent formatting from different members of the team.

Both clients may receive legally excellent advice. Only one has a reason to refer the firm to a colleague or to consolidate more work with it.

Process as a differentiator

For a transactional firm of thirty to seventy fee earners, the operational disciplines that drive client experience are not difficult to identify. They are difficult to sustain consistently across every team member, every matter and every deal stage without the right infrastructure. A partner who is personally very responsive and communicative cannot guarantee that standard across an associate team under pressure.

Process-level discipline, supported by tools that make responsiveness the path of least resistance rather than the result of individual effort, is what converts an aspiration to deliver excellent client experience into a consistent operational reality.

Where technology makes the difference

The parts of client experience that technology can most directly support are the ones that generate the most friction in the current model: first draft turnaround, document version management, counterparty review and the communication of status updates. A firm that has solved these operationally is in a better position to focus partner and senior associate attention on the strategic and relationship aspects of the client engagement rather than on managing the document process.
