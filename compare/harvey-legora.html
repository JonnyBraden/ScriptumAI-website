An honest comparison

Harvey and Legora are serious products. Both are used by some of the largest law firms and legal departments in the world, both have invested heavily in agents, and both now work inside Word. If your team runs litigation, research and regulatory work alongside its deals, a broad platform may well be the right choice.

Scriptum is built for a narrower brief. We serve transactional teams: corporate, commercial, property and employment lawyers whose work is drafting, negotiating and closing documents. There are no courtroom features you will never touch. That focus shapes where Scriptum works, what it drafts from, what it remembers and how we price it.

Who pays for the heaviest users?

General-purpose legal AI serves litigators and deal lawyers on the same platform, but they do not use it the same way. A litigation team reviewing a large disclosure set, or running research across thousands of documents, asks far more of the underlying models than a corporate team turning a share purchase agreement or a commercial lawyer working through an NDA.

When everyone pays the same seat price across practice areas, our view is that the lighter users end up covering part of the cost of the heaviest ones. In most firms and legal departments, the lighter users are the transactional ones.

A flat seat price is only fair when everyone on it does broadly the same kind of work.

The vendors themselves recognise the tension. When Legora introduced consumption-based pricing in June 2026, it said usage "becomes increasingly variable across users, teams, and organizations" and that this variability "is exactly what seat-based pricing can't absorb". Its chief executive has said it no longer made sense for a light user to pay the same as the heaviest. Legora's answer is to charge by usage. Harvey has said it will keep seat-based pricing because it gives customers transparency and predictability.

Both are reasonable answers for a platform that serves every practice. Ours is different. Because Scriptum is built only for transactional work, our pricing is set around transactional use. Your corporate and commercial lawyers are not carrying the cost of a document review exercise in another department, and your budget does not move with someone else's busy month.

Where you work

Lawyers draft in Word. Harvey and Legora both offer Word add-ins alongside their main web platforms. Scriptum is built the other way round: the Word add-in is the product, with a matter workspace for sharing drafts with your client and the other side. No separate portal to persuade people to open.

What it drafts from

Scriptum drafts from your firm's own precedents and house style, not a generic model's idea of a contract. When a lawyer edits a suggestion or approves a clause, that decision feeds the next draft, so suggestions look more like your firm's work the more you use it.

What it remembers

Most legal AI helps you produce a document and stops there. Scriptum captures clause choices, negotiation positions and deal outcomes as a byproduct of ordinary drafting, and keeps them as a record that belongs to your firm or legal team. When someone leaves, what they learned stays. Nobody has to tag, curate or maintain it.

Pricing you can read off a page

Scriptum publishes its prices. From £79 per user per month, with no implementation fees, no minimum seats and no multi-year commitment. You can build the business case before you speak to us, and compare it fairly against anything else you are considering.

When Harvey or Legora may suit you better

If your team needs litigation support, large-scale document review, legal research across jurisdictions or regulatory monitoring, a broad platform is built for that and Scriptum is not. If your work is getting documents agreed and signed, Scriptum is built for exactly that.
